Transformation: retain nonempty translations for ASCII single words, apostrophes and hyphenated words; prefer lowercase headwords for case-insensitive duplicates; preserve the source's Chinese and English definition fields; construct an inflection index from its exchange field; partition with FNV-1a. No definitions are generated by AI. The source dictionary can contain incomplete, dated or discipline-specific translations.

## Russian morphology: OpenCorpora

The extended form index uses the **Russian morphological dictionary by OpenCorpora contributors**, [opencorpora.org](https://opencorpora.org/), revision **417150**, obtained from the pinned `pymorphy3-dicts-ru 2.4.417150.4580142` data package. The package's metadata distinguishes its MIT Python code from the dictionary's **[CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/)** data license. Full original license: [docs/licenses/CC-BY-SA-3.0.txt](docs/licenses/CC-BY-SA-3.0.txt), also `data/ru/LICENSE-CC-BY-SA-3.0.txt` in the XPI.

QuickLex contributors' adaptation retains only forms from explicit dictionary paradigms whose normal form matches a Russian headword with a Chinese definition, converts them to form→headword references, preserves ambiguity and ё variants, and combines these references with Wiktionary's index. It does not predict unattested words, add Russian definitions, or generate Chinese glosses. This adapted index is distributed under **CC BY-SA 4.0**, a later BY-SA version permitted for adaptations by the original 3.0 license. Original attribution and license remain included. [Pinned package URLs, hashes and transformations](docs/russian-morphology-source.json); conversion code: `scripts/russian_morphology.py`.

`DAWG-Python 0.7.2`, by Mikhail Korobov with algorithms from Susumu Yata and contributors, is a **MIT-licensed build-only reader**, downloaded from PyPI with a pinned SHA-256 and imported from its wheel without installation. No DAWG reader code, Python runtime, data wheel, or platform-specific binary is bundled with the XPI. The dictionary format is documented by the pymorphy3 project's `opencorpora_dict.storage` and `wrapper` modules.

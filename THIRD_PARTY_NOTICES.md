## Russian–Chinese dictionary: БРуКС / BKRS.info

Russian–Chinese articles now come from **БРуКС**, the Russian-to-Chinese dictionary distributed by [BKRS.info](https://bkrs.info/p47), snapshot **2026-10-14**. These are direct Russian–Chinese articles, not inverted Chinese-to-Russian entries or English-pivot translations. The Wiktionary/Kaikki definitions bundled in versions 0.2.x have been replaced.

The official download page explicitly permits free use of the databases for any purpose. Its linked [distribution statement](https://bkrs.info/w/бкрс/распространение) describes the dictionary as open and freely redistributable. The source's original permission statement and an English translation are in [docs/licenses/BKRS-PERMISSION.txt](docs/licenses/BKRS-PERMISSION.txt), also `data/ru/LICENSE-BKRS.txt` in the XPI. This is the source's own permission, not an MIT or Creative Commons license. Definitions are attributed to **БРуКС / BKRS.info and its contributors**; each built-in Russian entry links to its BKRS page.

[Pinned source URLs, SHA-256 hashes, and sizes](docs/russian-source.json). The unchanged upstream gzip, `dabruks_261014.gz`, is included in release v0.3.0 to keep builds reproducible after upstream snapshot expiry. It decompresses to UTF-8 DSL with BOM. No upstream website HTML, analytics, audio, or images are distributed.

Modifications by QuickLex contributors, 2026: convert DSL formatting to safe plain text; preserve Chinese senses, pinyin, usage notes and examples; resolve reference-only articles with a bounded cycle-safe traversal; retain Cyrillic single/hyphenated headwords; normalize stress/case; add explicit OpenCorpora form references and omitted-ё spellings; partition into 1,024 shards. Conversion code is public in `scripts/build_russian.py`. No AI translations are generated. Source omissions and errors may remain; the dictionary does not provide IPA.

## User-imported dictionaries

The optional DSL importer reads only a file selected by the user and stores converted shards under the plugin's `quicklex` directory in the Zotero profile. Imported articles keep their own name and are not attributed to BKRS, Wiktionary, MIT, or Creative Commons. They are not uploaded, synced by this plugin, or bundled in public releases. Their use remains subject to their own terms. Supported input is uncompressed UTF-8 DSL or UTF-16 DSL with BOM; executable markup and external resources are never loaded.

## Russian morphology: OpenCorpora

The extended form index uses the **Russian morphological dictionary by OpenCorpora contributors**, [opencorpora.org](https://opencorpora.org/), revision **417150**, obtained from the pinned `pymorphy3-dicts-ru 2.4.417150.4580142` data package. The package's metadata distinguishes its MIT Python code from the dictionary's **[CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/)** data license. Full original license: [docs/licenses/CC-BY-SA-3.0.txt](docs/licenses/CC-BY-SA-3.0.txt), also `data/ru/LICENSE-CC-BY-SA-3.0.txt` in the XPI.

QuickLex contributors' adaptation retains only forms from explicit dictionary paradigms whose normal form matches a Russian headword with a Chinese definition, converts them to form→headword references, preserves ambiguity and ё variants, and supplies a separate form index for the БРуКС headwords. It does not predict unattested words, add Russian definitions, or generate Chinese glosses. This adapted index is distributed under **CC BY-SA 4.0**, a later BY-SA version permitted for adaptations by the original 3.0 license. Original attribution and license remain included. [Pinned package URLs, hashes and transformations](docs/russian-morphology-source.json); conversion code: `scripts/russian_morphology.py`.

`DAWG-Python 0.7.2`, by Mikhail Korobov with algorithms from Susumu Yata and contributors, is a **MIT-licensed build-only reader**, downloaded from PyPI with a pinned SHA-256 and imported from its wheel without installation. No DAWG reader code, Python runtime, data wheel, or platform-specific binary is bundled with the XPI. The dictionary format is documented by the pymorphy3 project's `opencorpora_dict.storage` and `wrapper` modules.
